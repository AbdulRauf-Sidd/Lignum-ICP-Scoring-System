ICP Scoring & Accounts System Architecture & flowPhase 1 build + AccountsJune 2026

Find, score and prioritise, then keep clients healthy

One system that scores prospects and monitors accounts.

Prospects are chosen by ICP, enriched across Creditsafe and Cognism, classified by sector, scored, reviewed and pulled into contacts on demand. The same records live on as accounts once they convert, carrying firmographic monitoring, client-scored quality, and per-client delivery efficiency from the CRM.

02

The flow, start to finish

1 · Import

Choose the ICP for the batch, then upload a CSV or add companies by hand. Rows are queued, nothing runs yet.

2 · Review & run

The validation gate checks each company with the free Cognism enrich call and skips those below a minimum-data threshold. The screen shows ready, to pull and skipped, with the credit estimate, then you press Run.

3 · Enrich & classify

One throttled batch: Creditsafe and Cognism enrich, Firecrawl and Exa infer the sector. Where the entity match is ambiguous, the company pauses into triage for the user to pick the right company. Each company is then scored against the chosen ICP, and the score is kept as history.

4 · Triage

Companies land in a holding area, not straight on the list. Confirm the entity where ambiguous, approve the proposed sector, and clear low-confidence or no-match flags before anything reaches the list.

5 · Work the list

Approved companies sit on the per-ICP target list. Open one for the full record, or pull its buyer contacts on demand.

Notification centre

Enrichment issues surface here: failed pulls, low data confidence, no-match flags, and contacts returned with mid or low email quality after a bulk pull.

Classification into triage

Firecrawl reads the company's own site as the ground truth for what they make; Exa corroborates and adds breadth. An LLM maps the result to your preset sector and sub-sector list against fixed rules, with a confidence score. Anything unclear drops to triage for a human, so the model proposes and a person approves.

Waterfall email enrichment

When a contact email is missing, n8n chains fallbacks: syntax match from known company emails, then a provider such as Apollo or Prospeo, still writing back into the contact. Cognism's own email quality grade is shown beside each email, sortable and filterable. A verification layer is parked unless bounce rates demand it.

Estimated in credits, not pounds. Neither Cognism nor Creditsafe exposes a balance or a knowable per-item cost, so these show in native units only: companies, Creditsafe reports and Cognism credits, no pound figure. Firecrawl, Exa and the LLM have transparent per-call pricing, so their actual cost is tracked in pounds. The app meters what it spends; that same metering enforces the spend ceilings.

Config: the ICP profiles and models

Weights, bands and tier thresholds sit in config, not code. A shared base holds the categories, the USD bands and the confidence rule; each ICP layers its own weights and fit rules. The account health model lives here too, on the same pattern.

08

Accounts

active clients
Optional module, same entity later stage. Accounts can be built now or deferred to a later evolution. An account is a company that converted, so it is a filtered view with client-only fields and its own health model, not a separate system. The lifecycle fields and account tables are cheap to keep in the schema now even if the module waits. It runs as a second n8n automation.

Build now, no new dependency

Firmographic & adverse monitoring. Reuses the Creditsafe and Cognism pipeline. Creditsafe live monitoring pushes adverse legal or financial events into the notification centre; else a scheduled re-pull and diff.

Qualitative ratings. Client-scored 1 to 5: quality of feedback, speed of feedback, attractiveness to sell. Manual, with a periodic refresh reminder.

Talent Insights. Headcount change, attrition, average tenure. No API, so manual entry with a refresh reminder.

Health model. Combines the above with the efficiency pillar, weighted in config, same shape as the ICP scoring.

Efficiency pillar Loxo, pending live key

Per-client delivery efficiency, reconstructed from Loxo jobs, candidates and placements and joined by company:

average fee · CV→1st interview · 1st interview→offer · offer→placement · job fill rate · time to hire

Effort versus return. Fee per CV sent is the headline, revenue per unit of effort. Time to hire shows both raw and as a throughput-adjusted fee, so a fast fill beats a slow one of similar value.

Definitions and schema are set now; the fields are confirmed on a live Loxo key before this pillar goes live.

09

Access, security & controls

how it is kept safe

Access

Roles

Two roles, admin and standard, enforced by row-level security. Shared: the target and account lists. User-specific: triage, the home page, and a user's own analytics and usage. Admin only: config, and the overall and per-user analytics and usage.

Throughput

Queueing

The queue lives in Postgres, drained to n8n in chunks at a bounded concurrency, resumable across executions, so a base-tier n8n plan is not overrun. Redis is not needed at these volumes.

Cost

Runaway control

A batch size cap, per-run and per-day spend ceilings checked before each paid call, idempotency so retries cannot re-charge, bounded retries, and LLM guards: a cheap pinned model, a token cap, one attempt, a monthly cap. The metering that shows spend also enforces it.

Security

Secrets & breach

Keys server-side only, never in the codebase or its git history. Row-level security, and login locked to the Lignum tenant. Scraped content is untrusted: the classifier is confined to schema-locked output so a crafted page cannot hijack it. The n8n webhook is secured to the app.

Data

Personal data

Contact data is Lignum's, under their existing agreements and policy. The app keeps it out of plaintext logs, behind the access model, and supports deleting a contact on request.

Delivery

Build practices

Separate dev, staging and production with fixtures so building never spends live credits. Structured logging and an error tracker, with alerts when a ceiling trips or a run fails. Scoring-engine unit tests. A handover runbook and a warranty window.
